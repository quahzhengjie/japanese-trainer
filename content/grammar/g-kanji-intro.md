**Kanji** are characters borrowed from Chinese, used alongside hiragana and
katakana. Each kanji carries a *meaning* and one or more *readings*.

**On-yomi and kun-yomi**

Most kanji have two kinds of reading:

- **On-yomi** — the reading derived from Chinese. Used mostly in compound
  words (two or more kanji together).
- **Kun-yomi** — the native Japanese reading. Used when the kanji stands alone
  or takes hiragana endings (okurigana).

| Kanji | Meaning | On-yomi | Kun-yomi |
|---|---|---|---|
| 山 | mountain | サン | やま |
| 人 | person | ジン・ニン | ひと |
| 日 | sun, day | ニチ・ジツ | ひ・か |
| 食 | eat | ショク | た(べる) |

**A rule of thumb**

- Single kanji + okurigana → usually **kun-yomi**: 山 = やま, 食べる = たべる.
- Compound of two kanji → usually **on-yomi**: 火山 (かざん, volcano),
  日本 (にほん, Japan).

This is only a tendency — there are many exceptions, so readings must be
learned word by word. Furigana (small kana above kanji) help while you learn.
